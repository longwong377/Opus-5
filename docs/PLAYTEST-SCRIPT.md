# The 20-minute playtest — script for a human sitting

**Written per the 4i amendment (P0a). Two sittings are scheduled: after P2 (arrival + save)
and after P3 (a living day). The same script both times, so the two reports are comparable.**

Why a script: the owner is the only human this project has, and an unstructured "have a look"
produces a feeling. A scripted sitting produces *comparable observations* — and the two
direction-changing findings this project has had both came from the owner looking at frames,
so this is the highest-yield instrument the project owns.

## Setup (before the clock starts)
- The build under test, its commit hash, and the date written at the top of the notes.
- Screen-record if convenient. Notes beat memory; timestamps beat notes.
- Do not read STATE.md or the session log first — arrive as a stranger would.

## The sitting (20 minutes, timed)

**Minutes 0–5 — arrival.** Launch. Say aloud (or note) the first thing you notice, the first
thing you try to do, and the first thing that confuses you. Complete the arrival: customs,
identicard, quarters. *Note the moment you first feel you are ON Babylon 5 — or that you
never did.*

**Minutes 5–12 — go somewhere you choose.** No objective. Pick anywhere from signage or
memory of the show and try to reach it. Note: could you navigate without help? What did you
pass? Did anything make you stop and look? Did anyone or anything react to you?

**Minutes 12–18 — try to live there.** Attempt, in any order: eat or drink somewhere; watch
people for one full minute; find your quarters again; interact with three different things.
Note what worked, what was inert, and what you *wanted* to do that had no verb.

**Minutes 18–20 — the exit question.** Quit. Then answer, in writing, exactly these five:
1. What happened in this sitting that you did not cause?
2. Name three people you saw. (Names are on HUD/interaction — could you learn any?)
3. What would you do first if you had another hour?
4. What broke the illusion hardest?
5. Would you come back tomorrow without being asked? Why or why not?

## What the agent does with the report
Each answer maps to a track: Q1 → the absence gate and G3's incident rate; Q2 → L4/L5;
Q3 → G-track priorities (it is the single best signal for what to build next); Q4 → the
craft/robustness cap list; Q5 → the Definition of Done's last clause, measured the only way
it can be.

File the report as `docs/playtests/YYYY-MM-DD.md`, verbatim, unedited. The agent responds
in the next session with a written disposition of every observation — fixed, scheduled, or
declined with a reason — appended to the same file.
